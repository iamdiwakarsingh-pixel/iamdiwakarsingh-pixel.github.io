Paradox Theory: Navigating Dual Missions in Social Enterprise

Published: November 2026 • By Dr. Diwakar Singh
View or download the slide deck for offline reading: Download Slide Deck (PDF)

Executive Summary

Social enterprises are fundamentally defined by competing imperatives: achieving financial sustainability while maintaining steadfast fidelity to their core social mission. Under classical management thought, these tensions are often treated as trade-offs. Paradox theory, by contrast, invites leaders to view them not as an either/or dilemma, but as an interdependent "both/and" dynamic.

Key Theoretical Takeaways

  • Performing Paradoxes: Competing stakeholder demands and metrics (e.g., commercial margin vs. beneficiary reach).
  • Organizing Paradoxes: Structural tensions between decentralized grassroots teams and professional corporate governance.
  • Belonging Paradoxes: Conflicting identities between business managers and social activists inside the same enterprise.

Practical Heuristics for Practitioners

Navigating these organizational paradoxes requires dynamic equilibrium rather than permanent compromise. By embedding hybrid governance mechanisms and fostering paradoxical mindsets among senior leaders, enterprises preserve mission clarity without sacrificing commercial survival.
